\begin{englishabstract}%
  {Neural reconstruction of structured domestic scenes for robotic simulation}%
  {neural reconstruction, robot simulation, 3D geometry, DM-NeRF}

Image-based reconstruction can reduce the effort required to model environments for robotic simulation, but neural representations do not directly provide independent objects, collision geometry, or suitable support surfaces. This dissertation evaluates the transformation of an instance-decomposed DM-NeRF reconstruction into an explicit scene for Gazebo Classic. The study uses the DM-SR \textit{study} scene and evaluates view synthesis, extracted geometry, object individualization, and simulator operation separately. On 100 test views, the reconstruction achieved 41.1100 dB PSNR, 0.9875 SSIM, and 0.0260 LPIPS. The mesh F-score was 0.0480 at 1 cm and 0.7750 at 5 cm, while the COLMAP baseline achieved 0.0285 and 0.3721 at the same thresholds and spatial support. Separation by instance identifiers produced 13 objects and retained 95.844\% of the homogeneous faces. Because the dataset provides no official semantic floor label, a geometric rule selected a floor candidate, which was then regularized to produce a planar support surface. In Gazebo, the scene loaded successfully, one instance was manipulated independently, and a dynamic box remained supported at the tested point. The results show that, for the evaluated scene and protocol, the decomposed reconstruction can be converted into independent models that operate in the simulator. Geometric correspondence nevertheless depends on the adopted distance tolerance, and the pipeline does not infer physical properties or articulations.
