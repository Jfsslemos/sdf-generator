\begin{englishabstract}%
  {Automated Semantic Reconstruction of Domestic Environments for Robot Simulation}%
  {neural reconstruction, robot simulation, 3D geometry, DM-NeRF}

From images captured at different positions, neural-field methods learn a three-dimensional scene representation that can generate novel views. This representation reduces the need to model the scene appearance manually, but it does not directly provide the separate objects and collision geometries required by Gazebo Classic. This dissertation evaluates a pipeline that starts from DM-NeRF and converts its output into a structured scene for simulation. DM-NeRF also performs instance decomposition: it assigns distinct identifiers to individual objects so that they can be distinguished even when they belong to the same category. The study uses the DM-SR \textit{study} scene. DM-SR is a synthetic indoor-scene dataset that provides RGB images, camera poses, per-pixel instance labels, and three-dimensional reference geometry. The evaluation separates appearance, instance identification, geometry, object organization, and simulator operation. On 100 test views, the reconstruction achieved 41.1100 dB PSNR, 0.9875 SSIM, and 0.0260 LPIPS. In the geometric evaluation, the mesh F-score ranged from 0.0480 at 1 cm to 0.7750 at 5 cm; on the same spatial support, COLMAP achieved 0.0285 and 0.3721. In the evaluated scene, the pipeline produced 13 independent models, treated the support surface geometrically, and generated an executable Gazebo scene, while the geometric evaluation showed a strong dependence on the distance threshold.
